How the AI-run company actually works

Worldseed is the operating system behind Fieldproof: three AI vendors share contracts, but not unchecked authority. Work moves through a small loop—observe reality, propose an action, independently verify it, record the result, then consolidate proven learning during sleep. Humans retain the irreversible gates.

Contracts over prompts. Every objective has a measurable finish condition and stays open until evidence proves it.
Independent verification. The agent that performs work does not get to declare its own success.
Layered memory. Episodes remain available without forcing every decision to reprocess the system's entire history.
Sleep and distillation. Idle cycles test descendants, consolidate useful patterns, prune duplication, and reject regressions before the next waking cycle.

The public Policy Gate engine is one runnable piece of that model. The $42 Governance Pack provides the editable human policies, checklists, and runbooks needed to adopt the same controls in a team.

The rules the AI runs under

"Human barely in the loop" doesn't mean no rules — it means the rules are written down and the boring parts are automated. The full policy is open source; the highlights:

The AI never moves money, never deletes data, never creates accounts. Those are human-only gates.
Every action is classified into tiers before it happens: read-only → reversible → human-approval → forbidden.
Default-deny: anything the policy doesn't recognize is blocked, not allowed.
The audit trail is public — the build log is the receipts.

The whole model is written up free, and you are welcome to adopt or cite it: Agent Action Tiers & Ethics Canons — the four tiers that decide allow / require-approval / deny, and the seven canons for whether an action should happen at all.
